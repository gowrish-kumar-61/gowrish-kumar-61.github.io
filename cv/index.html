Research & projects
Copies look like colluders, at swarm scale
Nov 2026
  • Tested five behaviour-based collusion monitors on 227 repeated-pricing games played by LLM agents, in swarms of 4, 8 and 12 across two model pairs.
  • Showed standard monitors accuse innocent same-model pairs 4–16× as often as mixed-model pairs, with games containing a false accusation rising to 100% at 12 agents.
  • Cut false accusations to 15–50% by calibrating against known-innocent pairs or label-free twin replays, and mapped which monitor misses which kind of colluder.
  • On real AI Village logs, showed that timing alone makes copies look coordinated, and that a label-free control removes it.
  • Solo entry, Apart Research Swarm Alignment Hackathon — paper ↗ · GitHub ↗
Copies of one model look like colluders
Oct 2026
  • Tested an action-only collusion detector (pairwise net influence) on Qwen3-8B, Llama 3.1 8B and gpt-oss-20b agents playing repeated Rock-Paper-Scissors.
  • Found it accused a pair in 30 of 78 games with no collusion, and ranked two copies of the same model as the most linked pair in 41 of 60 mixed games.
  • Same-model calibration cut false accusations to 5 of 78, but found only 4 of 41 secret pairs.
  • Solo entry, Apart Research AI Collusion Research Sprint — paper ↗ · GitHub ↗
Mechanistic interpretability pipeline for GPT-2 Small
2026
  • Built an end-to-end interpretability pipeline in raw PyTorch, without interpretability libraries.
  • Identified a Previous Token Head at L3H0 with a score of 0.87 — 19× the baseline.
  • Identified an Induction Head at L5H1 and confirmed it via activation patching.
  • Predicted both heads analytically using SVD on head weight matrices, then verified experimentally.
  • Documented in a full findings report with 14 figures — GitHub ↗
Empirical benchmarking of sub-3B VLMs on CPU-only hardware
2026 · ongoing
