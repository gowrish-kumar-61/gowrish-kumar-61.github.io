what I've been working on
Copies look like colluders, at swarm scale

When an AI agent swarm is built mostly from copies of a few models, behaviour-based collusion monitors accuse innocent copies 4 to 16 times as often as mixed-model pairs, and false alarms grow with swarm size. I tested five monitors on 227 LLM pricing games and on real AI Village logs, and calibration cut false accusations to 15–50%. Solo entry to Apart Research's Swarm Alignment Hackathon.

Copies of one model look like colluders

Can an auditor who sees only what LLM agents do detect collusion? I tested an information-theoretic detector on Qwen, Llama and gpt-oss agents playing repeated Rock-Paper-Scissors. It accused a pair in 30 of 78 games with no collusion at all, and kept flagging two copies of the same model as the most linked pair. Solo entry to Apart Research's AI Collusion Research Sprint.

Mechanistic interpretability pipeline for GPT-2 Small

Built in raw PyTorch with no interpretability libraries, because I wanted to understand every step. To test it against a known answer, I planted a previous-token head (L3H0) and an induction head (L5H1) in synthetic weights. It picked out the planted previous-token head clearly (score 0.87, 19× the baseline) but not the induction head, which only works when an earlier head feeds it. Running it on the real GPT-2 weights is next.

Benchmarking sub-3B VLMs on CPU-only hardware

Measuring accuracy, latency, and memory for SmolVLM, Moondream2, and TinyLLaVA on VQAv2 — CPU only. Nearly every VLM benchmark assumes a GPU, and that's not how edge or budget deployment actually works. It exposes real bottlenecks: Moondream2 takes 700+ seconds per image on Intel CPUs, which nobody reports.
